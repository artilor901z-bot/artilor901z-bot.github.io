Wired Coroner

Role — Design · programming · 3D art

  • Systems & gameplay
  • 3D modeling

You are a coroner — already dead. Build your own Cybernetic Autopsy System from wires and retro tech, and dive into cybernetic corpses to find the truth behind your own murder. After all, only the dead won’t hide.

MY PART

What I did

  1. 01

    Route map

    The node-based route map, from generation to screen: three fixed lanes, 4–5 layers per section, links that never cross, node types rolled with inherited weights and checked against pacing rules (a combat between two shops, a shop on every route), node states, saving and restoring, and the in-world monitor the map is shown on.

  2. 02

    Event system

    The whole map-event system: the event database and pool, the choice panel, a library of effects and conditions, editor tools that generate and validate events from data tables, and the localization table. All 59 events in the game are built with it.

  3. 03

    Event design

    Designed and wrote 15 of the world events myself (Ticket Spitter, Gig Wall, Stray Watchdog, Misdelivered Parcel…) and implemented the rest from the team’s design spec.

  4. 04

    3D props

    Modeled in-game props: the map monitor and the CD, U-disk and VHS storage devices.

Ticket Spitter

Two minutes before the last maglev pulls in, the platform’s ticket machine starts convulsing and spitting tickets everywhere.

The screen scrolls the same sentence: “Municipal Transit thanks you for your patience. Thanks you for your patience. Thanks you for your patience.”

On the platform there is only you, a sleeping drunk, and three cameras pointed in three different directions.

  • The cameras did not move. But after that night, you kept feeling that every streetlight dimmed a notch as you passed.

  • The machine went quiet. The apology on the screen was replaced by your credit number, followed by one small plus sign.

  • The drunk squeezed onto the last train with an armful of tickets and saluted you through the closing doors. Some luck is luck you hand over.

Gig Wall

Under the overpass there is a hiring wall, holographic listings layered and flickering like neon moss.

Nexa Group wants “Acid Rain Flow Volunteers.” Under compensation: Ration tickets, and honor.

Cynapse Network wants “Data Cleaners” contractor for the night shift. The pay is good while under requirements is blank. What’s at the bottom is a location.

  • You wiped down cabinets that had no fingerprints on them all night. The pay landed instantly, minus a “uniform depreciation fee.”

  • You held a traffic sign in the acid rain for four hours. The honor never came. The tickets did. The cough is yours to keep.

  • The client never showed his face. The payment slid out under the door: one unlabeled U-Disk. Your implant was scanned three times on the way home.

Stray Watchdog

Beside the trash compaction station, an early-model Nexa watchdog is still executing its last order: guarding a street number that was demolished years ago.

Its main cell has swollen. Its tail sweeps once every ten seconds. It scans you, blinks green twice, and tags you as “resident.”

The owner on file relocated to “address unknown” three years ago. The backup power cell, though, is full. Nexa redundancy. More reliable than the owner.

  • The tail stopped mid-sweep. “It’s only a machine.” You said that to yourself twice tonight.

  • It settled down beside the racks, registered the lab as “home,” and growled twice under its breath at every device that powered on.

  • It limped off toward the south side of the city. In the orders field you left a single line: patrol, range: any.

Misdelivered Parcel

A delivery drone sets an insulated box down at the lab’s back door, turns its confirmation light green, and flies off. Recipient: “7th floor resident.” The only thing in this building still drawing power is your lab.

The sender field is blank. The acid rain has soaked the shipping label down to a single barcode. Medical ice packs are taped to the inside wall. Still cold.

  • The counter clerk pushed the compensation voucher across without looking up. “Four hundred and twelfth misdelivery this month.”

  • Under the ice packs there were no organs, only storage media and a note: “Transcribe as-is. Don’t ask.” You did not transcribe it, and you did not leave it as-is.

  • Someone came at three in the morning and asked one question: “Did you open it?” You shook your head. The extra he paid was for that shake of the head.

Text and numbers are from the game’s localization table (November 2026 build). The game ships in English and Chinese, so the Japanese page shows English. The starting resources are for this demo only.
